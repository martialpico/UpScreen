Cupping therapy.

Therapeutic cupping lifts and decompresses tight tissue — the opposite of pressing in. By drawing the skin and fascia upward, it boosts local circulation, releases stubborn adhesions and eases muscular tension as part of your treatment.

Cupping can leave temporary circular marks for a few days — that's normal and painless. It's used as one part of a wider hands-on treatment, not a standalone fix, and we'll check it's right for you first.

How it works

A gentle lift,
not a hard press

Cups create a light suction that draws the skin and underlying fascia upward, decompressing the tissue beneath. That negative pressure encourages fresh blood flow into tight areas and helps the muscle let go — often paired with massage or myotherapy for a deeper release.

Cupping or massage?

Massage works by pressing into the tissue; cupping does the reverse, lifting and decompressing it. The two complement each other well, and your therapist will combine them to suit what your body needs on the day. About remedial massage →
